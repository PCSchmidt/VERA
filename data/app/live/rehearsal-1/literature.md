## Literature review

For covariate shift, the sources describe weighted conformal prediction as relying on a likelihood ratio between training and test covariate distributions [R2]. Another source states the assumptions more sharply: the conditional distribution of Y given X must stay constant, and the likelihood ratio must be known or very accurately estimated [R10]. The DriftGuard paper says importance weighting can restore validity under covariate shift when density ratios are known [R7]. The same paper reports that, under unrestricted conditional shift, the identified set for target coverage is the whole interval [0,1] [R7]. One covariate-shift paper claims near-optimal conditional coverage by adaptive prediction sets whenever the likelihood ratio is estimable at a sufficient rate [R4]. A further source gives a finite-sample guarantee for weighted conformal procedures only under bounded density ratios [R113].

For label shift, the evidence is thin. One source shows that label-weighted conformal prediction can give a finite-sample macro-coverage guarantee [R103]. One analysis of pseudo-calibration under a bounded label-conditional covariate shift model derives a lower bound on target coverage in terms of the source-domain loss of the classifier and a Wasserstein measure of the shift [R86]. The passages do not establish a coverage guarantee for label-shift-adapted conformal prediction with estimated label ratios.

For temporal drift, adaptive conformal inference is reported to achieve the desired coverage frequency over long time intervals irrespective of the true data generating process [R10]. For adaptive conformal inference, the unconditional guarantee concerns long-run coverage; the authors also show that when the distribution shift is small and the prediction algorithm takes a certain simple form, ACI additionally obtains approximate marginal coverage at most time steps [R10]. Conformal PID control states that its quantile tracking achieves long-run coverage under no assumptions except boundedness of the scores [R12]. The strongly adaptive variant is said to achieve provably small regret over all local time intervals of a given width [R77]. A heuristic ensemble method is explicitly said to offer no analogous regret or coverage guarantee [R26].

For non-exchangeable calibration with weights, the introductory review says weighted procedures for drift always satisfy marginal coverage and are exact when the magnitude of the shift is known [R6]. The same review notes that, in a weather example, total-variation distance between adjacent points could not be measured, so no formal coverage bound was available [R6]. The time-series EnbPI method gives coverage-gap bounds that converge to zero only under additional assumptions [R25]. A temporal knowledge-graph study ties a conditional coverage gap to local volatility under bounded local exchangeability [R66]. An applied study of lab-to-track thermal transfer reports that density-ratio weighting of EnbPI gave only a modest improvement in coverage, which the authors describe as not a complete fix [R44].

Overall, the passages do not support a single comparison across all four families. Guarantees are finite-sample for weighted covariate-shift methods given known or well-estimated ratios, and long-run or regret-style for adversarial online methods. Drift-discounted weighting depends on knowing or bounding the drift, and label-shift guarantees are poorly covered here.

## Retrieval and its limits

This review rests on 12 search queries, which retrieved 120 candidate papers. A relevance screen kept 35 and set aside 85; 6 were read in full text and 29 at the abstract only. Where this review says that something is missing or not established, it means that these searches did not find it: it is not a measure of the literature, and no figure for how much of the field the searches missed is available for a topic given without a list of its key papers.
